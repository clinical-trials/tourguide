# AI SF Tour: route and launch draft

Research checked September 8, 2026. This is a proposed operating plan, not a field-tested timetable. All walking distances, ride durations, and arrival times below are planning estimates. Current route availability is verified against SFMTA; actual reliability must be measured on the street.

## Recommended offer

Two regular, independently bookable walking-and-Muni tours, both beginning at Fisherman’s Wharf, Tuesday through Sunday. A separate sunny-day Branch C is proposed below; its dates and booking terms are not yet set:

| | Branch A — Neighborhoods, ideas, and downtown AI | Branch B — Builders, the waterfront, and the ballpark |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 8:30 a.m.–noon | 2–5:30 p.m. |
| Core route | Wharf → North Beach → Chinatown → Central Subway → Moscone area → Anthropic’s Howard Street corridor → Ferry Building | Wharf → Muni to Dogpatch → Corgi Cafe area → Pier 70 / Y Combinator neighborhood → Chase Center → OpenAI exterior → Oracle Park |
| Start | Proposed public sidewalk meeting point at Jefferson and Taylor; precise position and land manager requirements to confirm | Same meeting point |
| Finish | Ferry Building exterior | Oracle Park exterior |
| Walking estimate | Approximately 2.5–3 miles, with neighborhood inclines and station access | Approximately 2–2.5 miles, mainly along the eastern waterfront corridor |
| Intended audience | Ages 16+, curious visitors; no technical background needed | Ages 16+, curious visitors; no technical background needed |

The user’s proposed price is **$195 per person**, with **$40 per person for a guided Muni return to Fisherman’s Wharf**. This draft assumes $195 buys one 3.5-hour departure. Confirm whether the price instead covers both branches before accepting payment. The two distinct-route assumption also remains to be confirmed; the schedule can instead support a repeated single route if that is the intended product.

Fare policy: guests purchase their own Muni passes and fares. The tour includes practical instruction in using Muni. The optional $40 return pays for the guide’s time; guest transit fares are separate. Meals, cafe purchases, game tickets, attraction admissions, and gratuities are also separate.

Begin with a 4–6 guest pilot so the guide can test boarding, crossings, conversation volume, and restroom timing. Set the final capacity and minimum departure size from those runs. A premium price needs a knowledgeable guide, clear narrative, a useful local takeaway, and time for conversation; a list of office facades alone will not establish the value.

## Muni skills included in the tour

Teach each guest how to use the actual fare they hold before the first ride, then reinforce it at transfers. Cover the pass’s vehicle coverage and same-day expiry; Clipper tapping; MuniMobile activation/showing; paper proof of payment; station entry; route direction; and stop requests. At the finish, have guests plan one onward trip and retain access to their fare. Follow the sourced [passenger mini-lesson and guide delivery notes](AI-SF-Tour-Muni-Mini-Lesson.md). Help guests purchase their own fare if needed and explain a smartphone-free option. Public copy promises “We teach you how to enjoy the city the way the locals do”; teach the detailed steps in person.

## Optional guided return

The return starts after the advertised tour finish and adds time to the outing.

| Departure | Proposed return | Additional time estimate |
|---|---|---|
| After Branch A | F streetcar toward Fisherman’s Wharf from the Ferry Building stop; alight Jefferson / Taylor | 25–40 minutes including wait and walks |
| After Branch B | Walk to Townsend / Lusk or Third / Brannan; 30 outbound toward the Presidio; alight Columbus / North Point, then walk to the Wharf | 40–55 minutes, potentially longer during event traffic |

The F serves the Ferry Building and Jefferson / Taylor. It normally runs daily from 7 a.m. to midnight, with a 12-minute published midday frequency. [F Market & Wharves](https://www.sfmta.com/routes/f-market-wharves). The 30 lists the afternoon return stops above in its outbound direction. [30 Stockton](https://www.sfmta.com/routes/30-stockton).

A guest staying for a game should not book the immediate post-tour Wharf return. A later post-game escort would require its own staffing and price.

As checked on September 8, SFMTA lists adult fares of $2.85 by Clipper / credit or debit tap, or $3 cash, valid for 120 minutes including transfers. Its Muni-only day pass is $5.70. Ages 18 and under ride regular Muni free; discounted fares apply to eligible older and disabled riders. Cable cars use a separate fare and are not part of this plan. SFMTA also says single-ride passes stopped being available in MuniMobile on September 1, 2026; avoid old instructions telling guests to buy that product. Select the simplest available payment method in the pilot. [Current Muni fares](https://www.sfmta.com/getting-around/muni/fares).

## Branch C: sunny-day west-side special

**Public concept: “When the fog takes a day off.”** This is a separate walking-and-Muni outing, offered only on sunny days when Ocean Beach is free of fog. It does not add western stops to Branch A or B. South Bay locations remain storytelling topics, not visits.

| Proposed sequence | Experience |
|---|---|
| Ocean Beach | Shore-side NorCal surf culture: wetsuits, not board shorts. Start with the beach condition that determines whether the special runs. |
| Golden Gate Park and Japanese Tea Garden | Garden visit; confirm entry, admission inclusion and time for a group before publishing dates. |
| Haight-Ashbury | Counterculture, creative communities and neighborhood stories. |
| Japantown | A distinct cultural neighborhood and a proposed place to linger after the outing. Exact finish point to confirm. |

The west-to-east sequence is a planning proposal, not a measured itinerary. Trial the transit legs, walking, garden visit, restrooms and breaks with the intended group. Set the duration from that trial; do not advertise all four stops as another 3.5-hour departure. Price, starting time, meeting point, capacity, inclusions and any Wharf return service remain undecided. The $195 price and $40 return shown for A/B do not automatically apply to C.

**Weather operating proposal:** announce only selected Tuesday–Sunday dates, keeping Mondays closed. The guide makes the go/no-go decision using conditions at Ocean Beach itself, not sunshine downtown or a general city forecast. Confirm sun and absence of beach fog before departure. Before accepting reservations, publish the confirmation deadline, how guests receive the decision, and the cancellation/rescheduling/refund treatment if fog arrives or the outing cannot run. No automatic weather-based booking or weather monitoring is currently implemented.

C needs a separate staffing decision: use an additional guide or explicitly replace a regular departure after adjusting its availability. Do not book one guide for C while the same guide is committed to A or B. The current workbook and sign cover the regular A/B offer; C revenue, costs and weather cancellations are not included in the budget.

Source notes from the September 8 research: verify seasonal garden hours and entry arrangements before scheduling. [Garden hours](https://gggp.org/visit/admissions-hours/). Ocean Beach storytelling stays on shore; this concept does not include surf or swim instruction. [Ocean Beach visitor information](https://www.nps.gov/goga/planyourvisit/oceanbeach.htm).

## Launch work, in order

1. **Resolve the offer and field-test it.** Confirm distinct routes versus repeated departures, what $195 covers, in-tour fares, precise meeting points, cafe access, group size, cancellation terms, and whether the return runs after the stated finish. Run Branch A and B on a weekday and a weekend with a realistic group. Record every walk, wait, boarding, station entrance, accessible alternative, restroom, crowd pinch point, and total finish time. Repeat any leg that fails its allowance; remove a stop before publishing a timetable that depends on rushing.
2. **Confirm the business and route permissions.** Register the business through the SF Treasurer & Tax Collector; its current page states registration is generally required within 30 days of starting business. Bring this specific route map and recurring small-group format to the Office of Small Business and the relevant land managers. The Port’s event guidance covers ticketed events and waterfront use, so obtain a determination for the proposed meeting and interpretation spots rather than assuming the group is exempt because it is small. Arrange appropriate operator insurance and any venue agreements as part of that process. [Business registration](https://sftreasurer.org/business/register-business), [Small Business counseling](https://www.sf.gov/free-small-business-counseling-office-small-business), [Port event guidance and contact](https://www.sfport.com/specialevents).
3. **Prepare the guide and booking experience.** Build a short, sourced story deck; establish permission to use photos; keep the guide’s company-location notes dated. Check Muni alerts and both stadium calendars before each departure. Publish confirmed inclusions, walking demands, useful layers/shoes/water advice, exact finish and return arrangements, and an easy way to choose a branch and date. If a future route enters GGNRA land such as Ocean Beach, obtain the applicable NPS commercial authorization before operating there. [GGNRA commercial authorization](https://www.nps.gov/goga/learn/management/cua.htm).

The Heritage Inspirations reference is useful for information architecture: put departure times, price, practical preparation, inclusions, meeting details, difficulty, and the narrative itinerary close to booking. Use original SF copy and visuals, and keep its e-bike equipment, New Mexico seasonality, and operating policies out of this offer. [Reference tour](https://www.heritageinspirations.com/city-different-ebike-tour).
