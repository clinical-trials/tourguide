# AI SF Tour — delivery costs and cost reduction

Prepared September 14, 2026. Companion to the six-week paid pilot plan. All amounts are planning assumptions unless a current source is identified. The proposed $100 pilot price has not been activated on the website.

## Cost structure

For management decisions, separate direct tour delivery from transaction/selling costs, operating overhead, and initial setup. Owner time is included at an economic value of $40/hour even if the owner defers payment; this is not a tax-bookkeeping classification of an owner's draw.

At four guests paying $100 each:

| Direct delivery input | Assumption | Cost per departure |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Guiding, preparation, check-in and closeout | 4.5 hours × $40 | $180.00 |
| Guide's Muni fare | One day pass | $5.70 |
| Consumables and printed materials | $2 allowance per guest | $8.00 |
| **Direct delivery total** | | **$193.70** |

Guide labor represents approximately 93% of this direct delivery total. If employees are hired, add applicable employer payroll costs, workers' compensation, overtime, and benefits rather than treating $40 as an all-inclusive employee cost. SF's minimum wage is $19.61/hour effective July 1, 2026. [Official SF wage history](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/2026_Historical_San_Francisco_Minimum_Wage_Rates_M9ynRNo.pdf).

The fare uses the current $5.70 day pass. Guests purchase their own fares, coffee, food and admissions directly, so these purchases are not included in operator delivery costs. [SFMTA fares](https://www.sfmta.com/getting-around/muni/fares).

Track the following separately:

- Payment processing: $12.80 for four separate $100 domestic-card transactions at 2.9% + $0.30. International cards and currency conversion can cost more. [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing).
- Service-recovery reserve: $12, or 3% of $400 revenue. This is a planning set-aside, not an incurred COGS expense, and it does not replace keeping funds available for undelivered-booking refunds.
- Selling expenses: referral commissions, ads, and time spent acquiring bookings. Include the actual channel costs without double-counting any processing bundled into a channel's fee.
- Overhead: insurance, general administration, website/phone costs and other ongoing business expenses. For this management model, these are outside the narrow direct-delivery subtotal.
- Initial setup: permits, registration, initial materials and equipment, and launch preparation. These still have to be recovered even though they are outside a departure's direct-delivery cost.

A $400 departure leaves $181.50 after direct delivery, domestic card processing and the modeled reserve. That is contribution toward overhead and profit, not final business profit.

## Prioritize these savings

1. **Fill existing departures before opening more dates.** Keep the eight-person cap and concentrate initial demand on the two pilot days. Six guests instead of four reduce direct delivery cost per guest from approximately $48.43 to $32.95. The two extra tickets add $183.60 contribution per departure after modeled incremental supplies, processing and reserve, before additional acquisition expense. Higher occupancy reduces cost per guest; it does not reduce total cash spending on that departure.
2. **Reduce preparation time while retaining the 3.5-hour guest experience.** Reuse a route script, departure checklist, passenger instructions and confirmation templates. Reducing total departure work from 4.5 to four hours saves $20 per departure and $240 across 12 departures. Rehearsals and time logs must establish that the shorter preparation allowance is realistic.
3. **Compare total acquisition costs.** Begin with direct bookings and a small number of productive referral partners. An illustrative 20% commission would cost $80 on a $400 booking; this is an assumed example, not a quoted platform rate. Include owner selling time and every actual fee when comparing channels. Do not cut a channel solely because it charges commission if it produces profitable incremental bookings.
4. **Use digital visitor materials.** A mobile route map, onward-transit instructions and neighborhood recommendations can replace most handouts. A target reduction from $2 to $0.50 in consumables per guest saves $72 at 48 guests. The original $2 is an allowance to test, not established spending.
5. **Standardize administration.** Start with one calendar, one capacity ledger and reusable booking/refund messages. Reducing weekly administration from three to two hours saves $240 across six weeks. Use paid automation only when its time savings or error prevention justify the total cost. Capture any extra setup hours separately.
6. **Keep routing and café stops predictable.** Rehearse transit connections, confirm the coffee service option and have a nearby alternative. Avoid operator minimum-spend commitments while keeping guest-paid purchases clear. Reduced queues save money only if they reduce actual paid time or prevent overtime; otherwise they improve the experience within the same labor budget.
7. **Schedule optional services to demand.** Prebook the guided return and account for its extra guide time. Keep Branch C separately scheduled and weather-dependent rather than allocating daily standby hours. Do not rely on optional return sales to subsidize an otherwise unprofitable core tour.

## Measurable pilot savings

The baseline six-week pilot has 12 completed departures, 48 paying guests, $4,800 revenue and a $558 operating surplus after owner operating labor and the modeled reserve, before initial setup costs and income taxes.

| Target improvement | Six-week saving |
| --- | ---: |
| Save 30 minutes of preparation per departure at $40/hour | $240 |
| Save one administrative hour per week at $40/hour | $240 |
| Reduce consumables by $1.50 for each of 48 guests | $72 |
| **Potential total** | **$552** |

If all three improvements are demonstrated, with all other assumptions unchanged and no offsetting new costs, operating surplus becomes $1,110 and operating margin becomes 23.1%, compared with 11.6% in the baseline. Reducing owner hours improves economic surplus; it improves bank cash only to the extent those hours would otherwise have been paid. These are targets, not achieved savings.

The baseline $40/hour owner-time allowance should remain visible. The principal opportunity is reliable, well-attended departures with efficient preparation, rather than assuming the owner's labor is free. Protect the quality of guiding, essential insurance and safe operation when evaluating savings.
