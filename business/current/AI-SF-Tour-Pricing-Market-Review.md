# AI SF Tour: what can the SF / Fisherman’s Wharf market bear?

Updated September 15, 2026. Review edition; no ticket price or sales setting changed.

**Recommendation:** keep $195 as the premium shared-tour target and use $225 as the next controlled upper-price test. The current offer has no demonstrated demand at either price. Keep the limited $149 founding pilot as the first learning step. Do not build the base business case around $249–$295 shared tickets.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $195 | 48 | $4,848.96 | $2,858.96 |
| $225 | 42 | $4,947.45 | $2,957.45 |
| $225 | 40 | $4,528.60 | $2,538.60 |
| $249 | 36 | $4,503.92 | $2,513.92 |
| $275 | 32 | $4,358.80 | $2,368.80 |
| $295 | 28 | $3,859.86 | $1,869.86 |

**$225 must retain about 86.5% of the $195 attendance** at equal costs. Compared with 48 guests at $195, 42 at $225 preserve contribution; 40 do not. These attendance declines are illustrative, not demand predictions. Fewer than 48 cannot run all 12 dates at the four-person minimum. This stress model retains every date’s budgeted costs; actual canceled-date costs must be reconciled.

At $225 and 48 guests, a hypothetical extra 20% sales commission reduces operating surplus to $4,044 and profit after setup to $2,054. Card/marketing costs remain in that stress case. Compare contribution per offered date and per qualified inquiry, not price alone.

| $1195 / 6 guests | 7.5 | $8,057.94 | $6,067.94 | 7 |

Private hours include 3.5 guest hours plus preparation/customization/closeout; corporate allows more preparation. Also retain three admin hours weekly, $900 overhead, guide transit for all 12 dates, $2 materials per guest, one domestic-card payment per party and the recovery reserve. Startup assumptions need confirmation for the new product.

Twelve $795 bookings produce $2,559.14 after setup. Only six bookings with all 12 dates’ costs committed produce a $1,879.63 loss. A private booking must not be counted on top of a shared tour it replaces: six $195 shared seats can contribute more than one $795 private party after direct modeled costs.

## Decision rule

1. Complete readiness and the six-week $149 Branch B pilot. Track all offered dates, actual hours, paid guests, refunds, acquisition and value feedback.
2. If the stronger attendance and quality/economics goals pass, test four comparable dates at $195. Seek 24 paid guests, at least 15 value responses with 80% positive, reliable timing and a 20%+ actual operating margin.
3. If that block passes, test four comparable dates at $225. Against 24 prior guests, at least 21 retain contribution at unchanged costs. Keep each date’s four-person minimum and the same quality/margin gates.
4. Do not assume $249–$295 is supported. Test a clearly defined new value proposition and audience before raising further. Private proposals require paid buyer validation and separate costing.

These small tests are directional. No market ceiling, customer conversion rate or annual demand has yet been measured. The next decision is a controlled price test, not an automatic website increase.
