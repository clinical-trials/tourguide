# OpenAI + Anthropic | Two neighbors, big questions

AI SF Tour / Original spoken script / checked September 14, 2026

About five minutes, plus brief guest responses. Use once within an existing A or B stop; replace the prior story rather than extending the route.

Look around San Francisco and you can see the physical side of AI: office buildings, sidewalks, coffee shops and people going to work. Two of the companies in this story are OpenAI and Anthropic. Their best-known assistants are ChatGPT and Claude. Who here has tried either one?

*[Pause for a show of hands.]*

OpenAI introduced itself in 2015. On November 30, 2022, it released ChatGPT as a research preview: a conversational interface where you could ask a question and follow up. That is an important change to notice. You could begin by describing what you wanted in ordinary language. [39,27]

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by a team that had worked at OpenAI, including siblings Dario and Daniela Amodei. It introduced Claude in March 2023. These companies have connected roots, even as they have developed their own products and approaches. [40,41,42]

Both offer assistants for tasks such as writing, explaining ideas and working with code. A useful comparison goes beyond which answer sounds more polished. How should the assistant behave when instructions are unclear, when it lacks evidence, or when a request could hurt someone? [27,42]

OpenAI publishes a Model Spec describing intended model behavior. It explains how the assistant should handle competing instructions and balance helping people with preventing harm. Think of it as a public statement of how the company wants its assistant to act. [43]

Anthropic publishes a constitution for Claude: guidance about the values and judgment it wants the model to develop through training. It also researches interpretability, which asks how these systems work internally. Both companies describe safety goals. Those published intentions give us something to examine; they do not prove that every answer meets the goal. [44,45,43]

Let us bring that comparison back to your visit. Imagine asking each assistant the same thing: "I am visiting San Francisco and want to use Muni. Explain how to check which direction to travel, and show me the official source I should consult before boarding."

What would make one answer more useful? Maybe it gives clear steps. Maybe it asks where you are starting. Maybe it identifies something that could change today. We can compare the answers, then open the actual SFMTA information and see whether the advice holds up.

You might prefer one explanation over the other. Keep track of what you preferred: clarity, a useful question, a source that supports the claim, or an honest statement of uncertainty. That is a more useful observation than simply announcing a winning company. A single prompt is a small demonstration, and a different task or version may produce a different result.

Now the bigger question: what would you want these companies to get right as AI becomes part of daily life? Helping people learn? Making work easier? Protecting private information? Giving people ways to question an automated decision? Pick one, and tell us why it matters to you.

*[Pause for two short responses.]*

That is why these companies belong on a San Francisco tour. The software reaches far beyond the city, but we can stand in the neighborhoods where part of that work happens and ask what it means for the people who live here. Builders, ballers and the Bay. And today, you get to bring your own questions.
