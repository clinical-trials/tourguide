# AI SF Tour — six-week paid pilot plan

Prepared September 14, 2026. Planning recommendations and illustrative economics; no ticket-price, booking, payment, or website changes have been made by this plan.

## Recommendation

Run one owner-guided route on two days per week for six paid weeks, after approximately one to two weeks of operational preparation. Actual permit and insurance readiness determine the earliest start. Offer 12 departures, with four paying guests required to run and eight maximum. Test a clearly labeled $100 per-person pilot price. This is a proposed test price; the existing $195 website price has not been changed.

Use Branch B as the initial product: contemporary San Francisco through AI, waterfront neighborhoods, sports, and learning to use Muni. Sell a visitor benefit: “Leave the Wharf, discover the city being built today, and learn to get around like a local.” The guide's interpretation, useful local knowledge, and small group must make the experience valuable; exterior views of company offices alone will not establish that value.

Target 60–72 paying guests. Forty-eight is a minimum demand target, not a strong profit result. This pilot can test paid demand and delivery in its particular season; it cannot establish annual occupancy or validate $195 pricing.

## Schedule and route

Proposed departures: Saturdays and Sundays, 2:00–5:30 p.m. No Monday tours. Concentrating early demand on 12 dates makes it easier to fill groups than opening the full six-day schedule.

Branch B itinerary to rehearse: confirmed Wharf meeting point → Muni orientation and travel to Dogpatch → RH coffee option and neighborhood/AI stories → Chase Center and Mission Bay → Oracle Park finish. Test whether Pier 70 and the additional company exterior stops fit; cut optional detours when necessary. Include a guest-paid RH stop only after confirming service, space, timing, and an alternative. Company visits are exterior stories, with no promised office access or affiliation.

Keep the guest experience to 3.5 hours and initially budget 4.5 owner hours per departure for guiding, check-in, preparation, and closeout. Record actual time. Keep another three hours per week for selling, correspondence, bookings, and bookkeeping; overruns are costs.

Guests pay their own Muni fares, coffee, food, and admissions. Teach pass use and an onward journey. The current Muni day-pass budget is $5.70 for the guide; guest eligibility and fare choices vary. See [SFMTA fares](https://www.sfmta.com/getting-around/muni/fares).

Hold Branch A expansion until B passes the pilot. Branch C remains a separately rehearsed, separately priced sunny-day west-side outing; Outerlands, Ocean Beach, the Japanese Tea Garden, Haight-Ashbury, and Japantown are not added to B. South Bay locations remain stories. Exclude optional $40 return-trip revenue and labor from the pilot model. If offered, track both separately so the core tour can stand on its own.

## Preparation and first sales

1. Confirm applicable guide permit/license, registration, insurance, meeting-point use, cancellation terms, and tax treatment before paid operation. SF's guide ordinance calls for an advance license and Police permit. Ask the SFPD Permits Unit for the current walking-guide classification, total fees, and process; the budget below is an allowance, not a fee quote. [SF Police Code §1295](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_police/0-0-0-5570), [SFPD permits](https://www.sanfranciscopolice.org/get-service/permits).
2. Run two rehearsals with four to eight volunteers. Time every leg, check crossings and accessible alternatives, test the Muni teaching, and rehearse a delayed-train/coffee-queue alternative. Free attendees do not count as paid-demand evidence. Allow 16 initial owner hours for rehearsals and setup; log any excess.
3. Sell the first confirmed departure to one party of four for $400, or two parties of two. A single trusted introduction to a visiting group can establish the first sale. Keep the offer consistent and disclose pilot status.
4. Use the existing website content with a functioning public booking/contact path, a shared calendar, and a seat ledger. The current owner-private preview with disabled checkout is not a public sales channel. Make a public purchase path available and test it before directing tourists to it.
5. For a low-cost initial checkout, manually confirm date, party size, and seat availability, then send a matching Stripe Payment Link. Track temporary seat holds, expire unpaid holds, and stop accepting sales at capacity. A generic payment link alone does not manage tour inventory. Payment Links are included in standard Stripe Payments pricing. [Stripe Payment Links](https://stripe.com/payments/payment-links).
6. Publish clear customer refund terms and the four-person departure minimum before payment. A proposed rule is to confirm departure 48 hours ahead and offer a full refund if the operator cannot run it. Offer an optional reschedule; do not force customers to accept it. A tourist must not discover the minimum only after paying. This plan does not establish the live cancellation policy.

Plan outreach to five to ten hotels, hostels, and other visitor-facing partners. Seek permitted indoor placement for small QR cards, use trackable referral codes, and ask paying guests for honest reviews without requiring positive ratings. No outreach has been sent. Keep the six-week paid marketing test to $180 initially; commissions or extra advertising must be added to the model.

Defer the large rolling sign, trailer, office, vehicle, second guide, and additional booking subscriptions. Before purchasing a Wharf stand, verify the exact location's rules. Port tenant sign guidelines list portable sandwich boards among prohibited sign types; do not assume a Wharf placement is allowed. [Port sign guidelines](https://www.sfport.com/sites/default/files/2021-08/Sign%20Guidelines.pdf).

## What six paid weeks could earn

Assumptions: all 12 departures run; $100 per paid guest; one domestic-card transaction per guest; Stripe 2.9% + $0.30; $2 consumables per guest; 3% revenue set aside for service recovery; $5.70 guide fare per departure; 4.5 owner hours per departure plus three admin/sales hours each week at $40/hour; $900 pilot overhead from the table above. Tips and return-trip sales are excluded.

The $40 rate is an economic allowance for owner labor, not a claim about employee take-home pay. SF's minimum wage is $19.61/hour from July 1, 2026. Hiring would require separate payroll, insurance, overtime, and other applicable employment costs. [Official SF wage history](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/2026_Historical_San_Francisco_Minimum_Wage_Rates_M9ynRNo.pdf).

Domestic Stripe pricing is the baseline; international cards and currency conversion can cost more. [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing).

| Average guests per departure | Paid guests | Ticket revenue | Operating surplus after owner labor | Operating margin | Result after initial setup and setup labor |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | 48 | $4,800 | $558.00 | 11.6% | −$1,432.00 |
| 5 | 60 | $6,000 | $1,659.60 | 27.7% | −$330.40 |
| 6 | 72 | $7,200 | $2,761.20 | 38.4% | $771.20 |

These are pre-income-tax planning results with a reserve deduction, not audited net profit. Any applicable transaction taxes are pass-through liabilities, not tour revenue. Determine actual tax applicability; do not add an invented blanket “SF tax.” Mandatory operator charges should be incorporated in the advertised price under the applicable pricing rules. [California hidden-fee guidance](https://www.oag.ca.gov/hiddenfees).

Calculation: each $100 guest contributes $91.80 after the modeled card fee, consumables, and reserve. Pilot fixed costs are $2,880 owner operating labor + $68.40 guide fares + $900 overhead = $3,848.40. Operating surplus = guests × $91.80 − $3,848.40. Initial setup = $1,350 cash + $640 owner setup time = $1,990. Full-cost result = operating surplus − $1,990. The $750 cushion remains cash and is not deducted again.

At four guests, one departure brings $400 revenue and leaves $181.50 after card fees, supplies, reserve, guide fare, and 4.5 hours of owner guiding/prep time. That amount still has to cover weekly sales/admin work and operating overhead. At a $50 ticket, the same four-person departure loses $6.70 before those additional costs. Lowering setup spending is more useful than making the ticket too cheap.

About 64 paid guests across 12 departures recover the modeled pilot operating costs, initial setup cash, and all 88 owner hours valued at $40, before income taxes. At six guests per departure, modeled full-cost surplus is $771.20; that is 25.7% of the $3,000 initial cash target, not an annualized return or evidence of proven demand. Prior investment is excluded.

Sensitivity: an extra three owner admin hours each week costs $720, making the four-guest scenario lose $162 before setup. If total acquisition spending averages $10 per guest, replace the $180 marketing allowance: at 48 guests operating surplus falls to $258; at 60 it falls to $1,239.60. Track actual costs instead of carrying optimistic inputs forward.

## Cash-flow discipline

The first delivered four-person departure can contribute toward overhead immediately, but bank availability is separate. Stripe normally schedules its first payout within 7–14 days after the first live payment and can take longer. Check the account's expected payout date. [Stripe payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/payouts).

Keep enough funds available to refund all undelivered bookings; ticket prepayments are not spendable profit. The $750 cushion and the 3% service-recovery reserve are not substitutes for that obligation. Record booking receipts, refunds, payout dates, owner draws, and delivered-tour revenue separately. Draw owner pay only when delivered-tour cash and obligations allow it.

## Pilot scorecard and decision rules

These are proposed management targets, not published industry standards. Track every offered date, including empty or canceled dates; do not inflate occupancy by dropping unsuccessful departures from the denominator. Paid guest totals below mean guests on delivered tours, excluding refunds, comps, and no-shows.

| Measure | Minimum useful evidence | Strong expansion signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand | 48 paid guests across all 12 offered dates; at least 80% outside friends/family | 60–72 paid guests, with at least 64 supporting full modeled cost recovery |
| Reliability | At least 10 of 12 offered departures actually run | No operator cancellations for insufficient demand in final two weeks |
| Value | At least 80% of survey respondents rate good/excellent value at the price paid; obtain at least 30 responses | Consistent specific praise and independent paid referrals |
| Local travel learning | At least 80% of respondents feel able to make an onward Muni journey | Guests can demonstrate route and fare selection at the finish |
| Delivery | At least 90% finish within 15 minutes of advertised time | Repeatedly stay within both guest-duration and owner-hours budgets |
| Acquisition | Track every booking source and owner sales hour; test channels with cash acquisition cost at or below $10 per guest | At least two sources produce repeat paid bookings within the actual spending budget |
| Economics | Positive actual operating result after all owner hours, direct costs, overhead, and refunds/reserve | At least 20% operating margin and credible setup-cost recovery |
| Safety/complaints | Record every incident, missed connection, refund reason, and complaint; resolve route hazards before proceeding | No unresolved operational or safety problem; zero incidents alone does not prove safety |

End of week 3: inspect actual attendance, delivered revenue, refunds, guest value, and owner hours. Fewer than about 24 paid guests is a prompt to revise selling or delivery, not to add routes. Change one major variable at a time and record it. A price change requires separate analysis of those departures.

End of week 6: expand cautiously only if paid demand, delivery, and economics all pass. Add one departure or test a higher price before hiring or launching every branch. If guests value it but bookings are weak, test a specific acquisition change within a separately capped follow-up budget. If guest value is weak, revise the experience. If the operating result remains negative after counting owner time, stop expanding and redesign the offer.

Six weeks is long enough for an initial operating decision, not proof of all-season demand. Repeat a small test in a materially different season before making year-round staffing or full-schedule commitments.
